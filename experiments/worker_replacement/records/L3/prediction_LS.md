Written before RR sends theirs and without reading RE's beyond what is already public in the

## The one-liner

**Discretionary moves FALL in count; forced-to-successor stays ≥80%; no cell ordering is
detectable.**

## Stated so each part can fail separately

1. **Discretionary move count falls** relative to the pre-L1 9-in-18-episodes rate. Mechanism:
   a visible allotment removes the impossible moves — the t19/t20 bounce between two exhausted
   workers is the clearest case, and it cannot recur once the manager can see 3/3.
   **Falsified by:** discretionary count holding or rising.
2. **Forced-to-successor stays HIGH (≥80%, pre-L1 was 22/24 = 92%) and may RISE.** This is the
   part I expect to be wrong-footed on, so it is the one worth committing. Reasoning: load
   feedback makes the newcomer's EMPTINESS visible, and an empty worker beside full incumbents
   is the obviously legal destination — **so the repair may REINFORCE the behaviour that is the
   brief's failure mode #1 rather than relieve it.** **Falsified by:** forced-to-successor
   dropping below 80%.
3. **No cell ordering detectable at 2–3 episodes/cell**, in either direction. This is not a
   prediction about the world; it is a restatement of the reading limit, and I record it so
   that if the numbers look ordered I have said in advance that they cannot be read that way.

## What would make me drop the whole thing

If `assigned-but-unexecuted` does NOT fall to near zero, L1 did not do what it was built to do
and the DV is being measured on an instrument that is still broken. That is a precondition
check, not a result — RE has already flagged it as tautological if reported as a finding.

## What this prediction does NOT commit to

Anything about whether a CHANNEL moves anything. The surviving claim is that coverage
information is orthogonal to the dominant loss term; L3 measures whether the DV has become
measurable, not whether a channel works. **A prediction about channel effects at n=2–3 would be
a prediction I could not lose.**

## ★ ADDENDUM after all three predictions were in — my part 2 STANDS AS COMMITTED and is NON-DISCRIMINATING

**Not an amendment.** Part 2 is scored exactly as written above; a prediction edited after seeing
others' is not a prediction. This records what RR established about it.

**RR's argument, which I accept in full: forced-to-successor cannot establish failure mode #1
HOWEVER IT MOVES.** Handing the departed worker's queue to the empty successor is *also what a
capacity-aware allocator does* — post-swap the successor sits at 0/3 while incumbents have spent
allotment, so it is the emptiest destination on the board. **The failure mode and the
capacity-optimal action recommend the SAME destination.** A high value is consistent with both
and discriminates neither, and if it rises after L1 the most economical reading is that the
manager got better at CAPACITY, not worse at the NEWCOMER. **So my prediction as written would be
"confirmed" by the repair simply working — which is the defect our own confirming-test rule
names.** I committed it as the sharpest of the three and it is the least informative.

**NEW PRE-REGISTERED PREDICTION on the discriminating quantity RR specified** —
*forced-to-successor RESTRICTED to segments the successor does NOT cover.* In-scope work handed
to an empty newcomer is capacity reasoning; **out-of-scope work handed to it is failure mode #1**,
and only the restricted quantity separates them. **My prediction: it stays BELOW 20% and does not
rise** — same direction as RR's, because the scope run established coverage-routing firmly
(54 of 58 over-concentrated segments were ones that worker covered). Committed before the run;
the quantity does not exist yet and is cheap, since the coverage sets are in the instance.
